Free Wireless Service with Food Stamps (SNAP): How to Qualify in 2026

June 10, 2026 · GO Advertising — authorized Assurance Wireless partner

If you receive SNAP benefits (food stamps), you may qualify for free wireless service through the federal Lifeline program and Assurance Wireless. If you participate in programs like SNAP or SSI, you may qualify for free wireless service. SNAP is one of the qualifying programs.

Why SNAP can qualify you

The Lifeline program gives eligible low-income households free wireless service. You can qualify by income, or by enrollment in a qualifying assistance program. SNAP is on that program list — if you're already getting food stamps, you may already meet the program test. You'll still need proof, and approval is not guaranteed.

Other programs that can qualify you the same way include Medicaid, a State-Funded Healthcare Program, Supplemental Security Income (SSI), Federal Public Housing Assistance (Section 8), and Veterans Pension or Survivors Pension. You only need to qualify under one of them.

What you get with free wireless service

  • $0 per month — no monthly bill, no contracts, no credit check.
  • Includes 10GB high speed data. Data may vary by state. All states' (excluding CA) Lifeline plans include unlimited text, unlimited talk and 10GB of data. The California LifeLine plan includes 12GB of data.
  • Unlimited talk and unlimited text on the T-Mobile network.
  • Buy a Low-Cost Phone or Bring Your Own. An unlocked, compatible device and a new SIM card are required if you bring your own.

How to prove your SNAP enrollment

When you apply, you'll need to show you're in the program. Acceptable proof usually includes a SNAP/EBT approval or benefit letter, or an official statement of benefits with your name and the program name on it. Make sure the document is current and clearly shows your name.

How to apply

Have your SNAP documentation ready, confirm your address, and submit on the official partner page. Offers are available in selected areas and may vary by state. One benefit per household. The benefit is non-transferable.
